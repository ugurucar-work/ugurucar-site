The concept of "influencer" has been undergoing significant transformation. Initially, influencers were individuals with expertise in specific niches, leveraging their knowledge to impact their followers decisions. However over time, the emphasis shifted and somehow influencers started to arise suspicion in consumers mind and even hate in some not so rare cases. It became random accounts with large follower counts always trying to sell you things. This change came with a cost: genuine engagement and trust. This evolution has pushed brands to reassess their marketing strategies on influencer marketing because influencer marketing as we know it, is dead.

1. ## The Role of Influencer Marketing in Modern Advertising

You heard about Influencer Marketing but don't have any experience in it and maybe you do not know every term and definition. This first part is for you because influencer marketing has become a key component in today's advertising landscape, offering brands a unique way to connect with consumers. By partnering with individuals who have established credibility and a loyal following, brands can promote their products or services in a more authentic and engaging manner. At least they used to be.

### The Rise of Social Media and Changing Consumer Behavior

The growth of social media platforms like Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube has significantly contributed to the rise of influencer marketing. Consumers, especially younger generations, spend considerable time on these platforms, seeking content that entertains, informs, or inspires. They often trust influencers who provide relatable and valuable content, making influencer marketing a powerful tool for brands to reach and engage with these audiences.

### Types of Influencers in Modern Advertising

Influencers can be categorized based on their follower count and reach:

- **Mega-Influencers**: Individuals with over a million followers, often celebrities or public figures. They offer extensive reach but may have lower engagement rates.
- **Macro-Influencers**: Those with 100,000 to 1 million followers. They have a substantial audience and can effectively promote brands to a wide demographic.
- **Micro-Influencers**: Individuals with 10,000 to 100,000 followers. They often focus on specific niches and have highly engaged audiences, making them valuable for targeted marketing.
- **Nano-Influencers**: Those with 1,000 to 10,000 followers. They typically have close-knit communities and can drive high engagement through personal interactions.

![](/images/blog/the-influencer-marketing-is-dead-long-live-the-influencer-marketing/02.png)

Brands are increasingly recognizing the value of micro and nano-influencers, as their smaller but more engaged followings can lead to higher conversion rates and more authentic promotion. More on that later.

2. ## Beyond Follower Counts

Its time we shift our focus from the follower counts to the more substantial metrics of engagement and trust. After all, having a massive following is like owning a flashy sports car without any fuel, impressive at first glance, but it won't get you very far.

### The Illusion of Follower Counts

We've all been there, scrolling through Instagram, stumbling upon an influencer with millions of followers, and thinking, "Wow, they must be influential!" But let's pump the brakes for a moment. A large follower count doesn't necessarily equate to influence. It's like assuming someone with a vast library has read all the books, it’s the engagement that tells the real story.

### Engagement: The Heartbeat of Influence

Engagement is where the magic happens. It's the likes, comments, shares, and genuine interactions that breathe life into a post. An influencer with a smaller, but highly engaged audience can drive more meaningful results than one with a massive, yet passive following. It's basically quality over quantity, much like choosing a few close friends instead of many.

### Trust

Trust is the bedrock of any relationship, not just in our daily lives but also between influencers and their audiences. Authentic influencers who are transparent about their partnerships and stay true to their values foster deeper connections. Their followers trust their recommendations, making them more likely to act on them.

3. ## Why Nano Influencers Are the Real MVPs

Nano-influencers (1,000 to 10,000 followers) and micro-influencers (10,000 to 100,000 followers) have emerged as powerful allies for brands aiming to connect with specific demographics. These influencers typically operate within focused niches, allowing for targeted messaging that resonates deeply with their audiences.

**If you want real results, look for nano influencers.** These are the people with smaller but highly engaged audiences. They might not have millions of followers, but they have something even better: trust. Their followers actually listen to them because they’ve built a genuine connection. Plus, they’re often more affordable and willing to collaborate long-term.

4. ## Best Type of Influencer (Do not Trust the Algorithm)

Let’s face it: TikTok is like that friend who promises you the world but disappears when you need them most. One day, you’re scrolling, and there’s this person with a viral video, dancing, lip-syncing, or maybe doing something utterly random thing. The next day? Poof! They’re gone, vanished into the void, reduced into atom.

![](/images/blog/the-influencer-marketing-is-dead-long-live-the-influencer-marketing/03.png)

### The Algorithm Doesn’t Build Communities, It Builds Hype

TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and other short-form video platforms (even LinkedIn and Twitter, now X) are great for quick entertainment. But let’s be real they’re not great for building real connections. Someone can go viral overnight, but if they don’t have a loyal community, it doesn't mean much. Influencers, on the other hand, are supposed to have a *community,* a group of people who actually care about what they say, do, or recommend.

### The Problem with Momentary Fame

Here’s the thing about algorithms: they’re unpredictable. One day, your video gets a million views, and you feel like the king or queen of the internet. The next day, your content gets buried, and you’re back to being a regular person with 37 likes. This inconsistency is bad for influencers and even worse for brands. If you’re working with someone who’s only popular because the algorithm decided to bless them that week, you’re basically throwing money into a black hole.

At the end of the day, these platforms are there to make money and to make money they need content creators to create and content enjoyers to enjoy, right? So to push content creators, they will reward consistency with high view counts, maybe millions. That dopamine rush will be enough for creators to post weeks to come, only to get few hundred likes.

And let’s not forget the followers. Sure, someone might have 500K followers, but if 400K of them are bots or people who followed them for one viral video, what’s the point? You need someone with real engagement, people who comment, share, and actually care about what’s being posted.

### How to Spot a Real Influencer

So, how do you avoid wasting your money on algorithm-dependent “influencers”? Here are a few tips:

1. **Check Their Engagement:** Are people actually commenting and sharing their posts, or are they just ghost followers?
2. **Look for Consistency:** Do they post regularly, or do they disappear for weeks after one viral moment?
3. **Community Matters:** Do they interact with their followers? Do they reply to comments or create content based on their audience’s interests?
4. **Niche Expertise:** Are they known for something specific, or are they just posting whatever the algorithm favors this week?

5. ## Success Stories: Brands Thriving Through Authentic Influencer Partnerships

In today's digital landscape, brands are increasingly turning to nano and micro-influencers to foster authentic connections with consumers. These collaborations often result in higher engagement rates and more genuine interactions compared to partnerships with macro-influencers or celebrities. Here are some notable success stories:

**1. Daniel Wellington's Nano-Influencer Strategy**

Daniel Wellington, known for its minimalist watches, revolutionized its marketing by partnering with nano-influencers. This approach led to a 7.2% engagement rate across posted content, 850,000 collective impressions per month, and a 35% reduction in customer acquisition costs. The authenticity of these influencers fostered genuine interest in the brand, resulting in a 44% increase in positive brand sentiment scores.

**2. Fashion Nova's Micro-Influencer Expansion**

Fashion Nova leveraged over 5,000 micro-influencers in the fashion realm to showcase its affordable yet trendy outfits. These collaborations resonated with their dedicated followers, leading to explosive growth for the brand. They are also one of the leading actors in UGC field, which is subject for another blog post.

![](/images/blog/the-influencer-marketing-is-dead-long-live-the-influencer-marketing/04.jpeg)

**3. Glossier's Authentic Engagement**

Glossier partnered with micro-influencers who were genuinely passionate about beauty. Through authentic content creation, these influencers introduced Glossier’s skincare products to their audiences, resulting in elevated engagement rates and heightened brand visibility.

**4. Airbnb's Inclusive Campaign**

Airbnb's [#WeAccept](https://www.ugurucar.work/blog/hashtags/WeAccept) campaign partnered with a diverse array of micro-influencers who shared their travel experiences, showcasing Airbnb’s commitment to diversity and belonging. This initiative heightened brand awareness and increased bookings in underserved communities.

6. ## Conclusion: The Future of Marketing Lies in Genuine Influence

The landscape of influencer marketing is continually evolving, with authenticity and trust emerging as the most important factors for success. Brands that prioritize genuine connections over sheer reach are better positioned to engage consumers meaningfully. By aligning with influencers who embody their values and resonate with their target audiences, companies can cultivate lasting relationships and drive sustainable growth. As the digital world becomes increasingly saturated, the power of authentic influence will undoubtedly play a crucial role in shaping the future of marketing.
